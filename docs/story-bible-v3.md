## 1. LOGLINE & FRAMING DEVICE

In a near-future AI space race, a rival power secretly builds a breakthrough
fusion torch drive, then commits the greatest theft in history: they tow Saturn's
moon Mimas — the heavily cratered grey ice ball dominated by the 130 km-wide
Herschel crater, the "Death Star" moon — all the way to Earth orbit, claim it,
and start mining its helium-3. But Mimas is a small rocky core wrapped in a thick
ice shell, and the tow broke something. An accident already happened up there,
and they covered it up.

**Framing: a PAST TRUE EVENT.** The story is staged as *"THE MIMAS INQUIRY: A
Declassified History of the Saturn Heist"* — an interactive documentary produced
by the fictional **Orbital History Project** (the nonprofit that archives weird
space stuff nobody else wanted to touch), dated **2051, nineteen years after the
heist** (Mimas captured 2032). Title-card tagline: *"Declassified 2049. Assembled
2051. Believed by nobody."*

**The narrator: DOTTY** ("Docent Over-Transparent Tour-bot Yapper") — an AI
archivist docent with the energy of a museum guide who has watched too many
true-crime docs. Goofy, self-aware, meme-literate, deeply unthreatening — the
comic cushion around serious physics. Dotty does NOT know the truth: it is an
unreliable-but-honest curator. Everything it shows is real recovered material;
its interpretation is cheerfully suspect. When sources contradict each other,
Dotty shrugs and hands the mess to the reader: *"You decide. I'm just a bot
with a badge."*

**Reader entry:** cold open on a glitchy recovered helmet-cam clip — 8 seconds
of shaky footage from the Saturn extraction ("THE 72 HOURS"), timestamp
corrupted, audio half-lost, cutting to black mid-sentence. Then Dotty: *"Hi! I'm
Dotty! That was leaked footage from 2032. The people in it all remember it
differently. Let's ruin some reputations together."* The reader picks a starting
archive — REN'S SHIP LOG / QIAO'S FLIGHT RECORDER / THE HEARING — and that first
jump is the first trust tilt.

**Recovered-footage conceit:** every past scene carries in-world provenance,
e.g. `RECOVERED: Helmet-cam 04 — Sgt. Q. [declassified 2049, audio 62% intact]`,
`RECOVERED: ARGUS simulation log #7,411,203 — the one that worked`,
`RECOVERED: Lucky-77 demo broadcast — 4.2M views, "it's just a nudge bro"`.
Damaged sections are part of the play: `[AUDIO LOST]`, `[FRAME CORRUPTED]`,
`[REDACTED BY THE COMPACT, lol, good luck]`. Contradictions live in the gaps.

## 2. LOCKED CANON — HARD PHYSICS & PLOT BEATS

*(Carried forward from v2. Every number below is load-bearing — the jokes only
land if the facts are straight.)*

- **Mimas:** 396 km diameter; surface almost pure water ice; Herschel crater
  ~130 km wide. About **1/9 as wide as our Moon, ~2,000x less massive**; surface
  gravity 0.064 m/s² (nearly jump-to-orbit). "A grey iceball the size of a small
  country."
- **Interior:** small rocky core (~200 km) wrapped in a thick ice/water shell —
  possibly a young subsurface ocean (real 2024 research).
- **The drive:** "the Long Torch", a fusion torch drive. Burns roughly **two
  million years of humanity's total energy output**. Demoed by nudging asteroid
  "Lucky-77" on a livestream (4.2M viewers, chat spamming "it's just a nudge
  bro").
- **The prize:** helium-3 implanted in the regolith by billions of years of solar
  wind — "enough fusion fuel for a century" — plus water ice and core minerals.
- **The trajectory:** gravity assists off **all four gas giants AND their moons**,
  plotted by the AGI **ARGUS** running **trillions of simulations** — change one
  burn by a second or one flyby by a kilometer and the whole cascade changes. No
  human could plot it. Key insight: **Mimas never needed to arrive slow — a fast
  arrival just means a larger capture orbit.** All it ever needed was to get swung
  out of Saturn and start falling inward; the giants do the rest.
- **The extraction (most dangerous part):** ripping Mimas out of Saturn's crowded
  moon system (Titan, Rhea, Dione, Tethys, Enceladus…) without perturbing the
  other moons — threading a needle; escaping Saturn's gravity well was the single
  biggest delta-v cost. Then the **Jupiter flyby**: precisely timed to shed energy
  and fall inward; mistime it and Mimas gets flung, captured, or tidally shredded
  in Jupiter's Roche zone, plus the radiation belts.
- **The shepherd fleet:** captured asteroids flying in formation as **gravity
  tractors**; **DART-style impactors** for trim (DART 2022 proved kinetic
  deflection — but moving Mimas 1 m/s would take **~ten trillion DART-sized
  hits**); **mass drivers throwing Mimas's own ice overboard as reaction mass** —
  the moon burns pieces of itself to travel. Crew names the shepherd asteroids
  after farm animals ("Goose is drifting again").
- **Parking:** Earth's tides shred fluffy ice inside **~26,000 km (the Roche
  limit)**; flexing starts well before that — the in-story cause of the fracture.
  Parked at a **distant retrograde orbit (~70,000 km)**, where Mimas hangs in the
  sky **two-thirds the Moon's width**. Nobody voted on it. It just showed up,
  like a roommate's couch.
- **THE SECRET ("the Herschel Slip"):** during the final braking burn, a **~40 km
  fracture** propagated from Herschel's rim deep into the shell. Official story:
  **"expected settling."** Truth: the shell is degrading daily. If Mimas
  fragments in Earth orbit: debris ring, **Kessler cascade**, the orbital economy
  dies. Possibly the first crack started during the Saturn extraction ("the 72
  hours") — months before the braking burn.
- **The cover-up is false and the story knows it.** Ren is sympathetic (protecting
  her crew, her program, maybe her life), but the narrative never pretends
  "expected settling" might be true. Dramatic irony is the fuel.
- **All characters are fictional.** No real persons depicted, ever.

## 3. TEMPORAL LAYER SYSTEM

Four named layers. Every scene header shows `[LAYER] · [POV] · [DATE]`.

| # | Layer | Purpose | Visual tag |
|---|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | **THE EVENT** | The heist as it happened (2031–2033): extraction, flybys, braking burn, parking, mining ops. The thriller. | Grainy recovered-footage frame; timestamp burn-in; mono hiss. `RECOVERED: …` cards. Film-sprocket chrome. |
| 2 | **THE RECORD** | Present-day (2051) Dotty explainer: what the physics was, what the documents say, what nobody can prove. | Clean museum-kiosk panel; Dotty avatar; footnote citations; "per the declassified files" voice. Teal chrome, folder tabs. |
| 3 | **THE AFTERMATH** | Future consequences (2040s–2051): the mining economy, the crack watch, who got rich, who got hearings, who got memes. | Faded overexposed anniversary-retrospective; news-montage cards; interview lower-thirds. HUD-bracket chrome. |
| 4 | **THE HEARING** | Sera Voss's 2034 committee testimony as its own recurring timeline. Reader acts as committee counsel. | Hearing-room transcript: line numbers, `Q.`/`A.` blocks, perjury-warning banner, gavel sting on reveals. Paper-card inversion. |

**Interruption rhythm:** Dotty's RECORD pop-ins can interrupt any EVENT scene at
tagged moments (director's commentary that can't keep its mouth shut).
AFTERMATH beats arrive as "where are they now" interludes between chapters.
THE HEARING recurs as a throughline (scenes 5, 7, 10); questions asked there set
flags that unlock contradiction payoffs later. **Rule: past scenes move fast
(heist energy), interruptions slow down (think energy). Never two interruptions
in a row; never more than two EVENT scenes without a layer break. Reader-paced,
~10–15 minutes.**

**Earth ticker (not a layer — a running chyron):** the US/EU "loser side"
scramble scrolls under EVENT and AFTERMATH scenes at key moments: analysts
panicking, emergency sessions, crash programs, *"everything can change in a
second"* energy. Goofy-bleak 2032 cable-news graphics that aged terribly, e.g.
`TICKER: "OFFICIALS URGE CALM." / "SECOND MOON VISIBLE TONIGHT, 'WEIRDLY BIG.'" /
"EXPERTS SAY DEBRIS RING 'UNLIKELY,' ADD 'PLEASE STOP ASKING.'"`

## 6. ENDINGS (as 2051 "historical verdicts")

Gating: primary = TRUST readout (Captain / Taikonaut / Defector scores from
scene choices); secondary = contradiction flags; tertiary = key items (Sc.6
memo). Ties break toward the POV the reader last sided with. Trust readout stays
**hidden until Scene 10** by design (prevents gaming). After any ending, Dotty
offers "re-open the archive" — New Game+ with flags carried as "prior Inquiry
notes" for contradiction hunting.

### Ending A — "THE OFFICIAL STORY"
- **Gate:** Trust→Captain highest; C3/C5 uncaught or buried (certified the
  9-minute log in Sc.7, wrote "all nominal" in Sc.8).
- **As history:** The Inquiry's published conclusion: humanity's greatest
  engineering feat; the Slip was "expected settling," contained and monitored;
  the helium-3 century proceeds. Ren gets a statue. Dotty, reading the plaque:
  *"She looks great in bronze. The end? Probably!"* Final RECORD card: 2051
  deep-scan "inconclusive."
- **Tone:** triumphant, slightly queasy — the reader *feels* the buried thing.

### Ending B — "THE TESTIMONY"
- **Gate:** Trust→Defector highest; pressed Voss's *opponents* (not Voss) in
  Sc.10; leaked Qiao's note in Sc.9.
- **As history:** The Inquiry endorses Voss: the leak detonates, the Compact's
  telemetry is subpoenaed, the world moves against the Compact. Voss gets a book
  deal and a talk show. Dotty: *"Justice! Accountability! Merch!"* Final card:
  the crack is real and being "managed" — by the people Voss now works for
  (unexamined).
- **Tone:** righteous, crowd-pleasing — with one unexamined loose end the reader
  chose not to pull.

### Ending C — "THE THIRD TRUTH" (secret)
- **Gate:** `caught_C3 && caught_C7 && caught_C8` + ≥2 other contradiction
  flags + pocketed the Sc.6 memo. Trust may be split — that's the point. The
  threshold number is never displayed in-UI beyond a hint.
- **As history:** The Inquiry's sealed appendix: BOTH stories are half-true. The
  Slip is real — the first crack started in the 72 hours (C3), the Compact towed
  a cracked moon and mined the fracture (C5). AND Voss runs her own operation —
  never on the bridge (C7), heard the Slip live on comms and sat on it for two
  years (C8); her testimony is a leveraged buyout of the truth — she waited
  until the He-3 market peaked, then detonated the story to crash the Compact
  and buy in cheap. Final document: her funding ledger. Dotty,
  uncharacteristically quiet: *"Oh. Oh no. …Anyway! History is complicated!"*
- **Tone:** the rug-pull. The reader was the only honest investigator in the room.

### Ending D — "HERSCHEL'S FALL" (dark)
- **Gate (two paths):**
  1. **Neglect:** trust spread thin / reader never certifies either timestamp in
     Sc.7 and never leaks in Sc.9 — nobody with power is forced to act; the crack
     grows unmonitored.
  2. **Overreach:** TESTIMONY-gated but the reader also caught C5 and chose the
     harshest sanctions in Sc.10 — the cornered Compact runs an emergency
     "stabilization burn" on a cracked moon to prove control. It doesn't stabilize.
- **As history:** 2049 — the fracture propagates; Mimas sheds a city-sized
  fragment into the 70,000-km orbit; debris ring; Kessler cascade; the orbital
  economy dies in a week. Dotty's last line is not a joke: *"The footage from
  that day is 41 seconds long. We play it without commentary."* Then, barely
  audible: *"…we should have certified the timestamp."*
- **Tone:** the one unscary-free zone. Earned dread. The physics was always
  loaded; the reader chose who held the gun.

## 7. TONE GUIDE — goofy-but-thrilling voice

**The voice in one sentence:** the heist of the century, told by an unhinged
true-crime documentary narrator who is also a space nerd who genuinely did the
math — delivering it as declassified history the archives finally coughed up.

**The contract:** goofy but never stupid. The science is REAL and treated with
awe; the joke is never "space is dumb" — the joke is "a bunch of primates did
*this*, on purpose, with spreadsheets." Punch up at institutions,
bureaucracies, and physics. Never down at people. Qiao is a person, not a
punchline; his conflict is the tragedy inside the comedy. **Unscary rule:** the
Kessler-cascade stakes stay real (that's the thriller engine), but the telling
stays playful — the bomb under the table is discussed with the energy of a
cooking-show host explaining the soufflé might explode. It might. It really
might. Anyway, next step: mining the soufflé. **Dread always gets a
pressure-release valve within three sentences** — except Ending D, which earns
its dread.

**Comedy placement:** Dotty, the ticker, and the footnotes carry the comedy; the
EVENT scenes play straight. Meme-awareness lives in Dotty/ticker, never in
Ren/Qiao/Voss's mouths. The narrator never explains the joke — the footnotes
wink; the narrator stays deadly serious about absurd things.

### Rhythm techniques (read everything aloud)
1. **Staccato, then roll:** short punches, then one long rolling sentence that
   topples. *"They stole a moon. Not a rock. Not a satellite. A whole moon —
   all 396 kilometers of it, Herschel crater and all, towed across the solar
   system like a shopping cart with a rocket strapped to it."*
2. **Rule of three (third one twists):** *"The cover story had everything: a
   technical term ('expected settling'), a confident spokesperson (Captain Ren),
   and absolutely no relationship to reality."*
3. **Repetition with a twist:** *"The moon burns pieces of itself to travel. It
   burns its own ice to cross the dark. It is, and the archives confirm this
   phrasing, eating itself to get here faster."*
4. **Bureaucratic bathos:** build huge, puncture with official language. *"It
   was the greatest theft in human history, and the press release called it 'an
   asset relocation.'"*
5. **The list that escalates past reason:** *"ARGUS needed a Jupiter assist. Then
   a Saturn assist. Then assists off Uranus, Neptune, three of their moons, and —
   per the recovered logs — 'one (1) extremely rude maneuver around Triton that
   we do not discuss.'"*
6. **Em-dash ambushes:** *"The braking burn was nominal — nominal in the sense
   that a 40-kilometer crack propagating through the ice shell is technically a
   thing that can happen."*
7. **Deadpan caption vs. unhinged narrator:** *ARCHIVE CAPTION: "Diagram 7-B:
   Parking orbit (stable)." NARRATOR: It was stable in the same way a piano is
   stable while falling off a roof.*

### Sample lines (voice reference for the prose writer)
- **Theft reveal:** "Somebody stole a moon. Not 'moved.' Not 'nudged.' Stole —
  towed it a billion kilometers sunward, parked it next to Earth, and started
  strip-mining it for helium-3 before anyone finished their coffee."
- **Death Star (use max twice):** "Mimas is 396 kilometers of grey cratered ice
  with a 130-kilometer impact crater punched in its face, which means it has
  looked like the Death Star since approximately forever, and nobody — *nobody*
  — thought to guard it. You don't lock up the Death Star. The Death Star locks
  up *you*. They counted on this."
- **Ten trillion DARTs:** "NASA's DART mission proved you can shove an asteroid
  with a spacecraft. Great. To move Mimas one meter per second that way, you'd
  need ten trillion DARTs — which is less a mission architecture and more a
  confession that you need a bigger idea."
- **The Long Torch:** "The bigger idea was the Long Torch: a fusion drive that
  burns, per the declassified estimates, roughly two million years of humanity's
  total energy output. It is the most expensive lighter ever built, and they
  used it to steal a moon."
- **Roche limit:** "Park a moon too close to Earth and tides rip it apart — the
  Roche limit, about 26,000 kilometers up, the altitude at which physics files
  for divorce. Cross it and your moon becomes a ring. A very pretty, extremely
  career-ending ring."
- **Parking orbit:** "So they parked it far outside the kill zone: a distant
  retrograde orbit, 70,000 kilometers out, where Mimas hangs in the sky
  two-thirds the width of the Moon. Earth got a second moon. Nobody voted on
  it. It just showed up, like a roommate's couch."
- **ARGUS:** "No human plotted the trajectory. A human *can't* — it threads
  gravity assists off all four gas giants and a selection of their moons, and
  changing one burn by one second rewrites the entire cascade. ARGUS ran
  trillions of simulations to find the one thread through the maze. The station
  AGI did the math. The humans just signed the paperwork and tried to look
  calm."
- **The Herschel Slip:** "During the final braking burn, a forty-kilometer
  fracture tore outward from Herschel's rim and dove deep into the ice shell.
  The official statement called it 'expected settling.' The ice shell, for the
  record, had not been consulted, and did not agree."
- **Ren:** "Captain Ren is a professional. She tells the official story the way
  a vault door closes: completely, finally, and with the clear implication that
  asking again would be embarrassing for *you*."
- **Qiao:** "Co-pilot Qiao's flight record contains a 72-hour gap labeled only
  'Saturn extraction operations.' Somewhere in those three days, the first crack
  started. He has never said when. His smile, in every recovered photo from
  that week, is doing a lot of heavy lifting."
- **Voss:** "Defector Sera Voss sat before the committee and contradicted the
  official story point by point, with exhibits, with timestamps, with the serene
  energy of a woman who had been waiting years to say 'per my last email' to a
  government."
- **Fast-arrival insight:** "Here's the trick the other side missed: Mimas never
  needed to arrive slow. A fast arrival just means a bigger capture orbit — you
  don't brake harder, you brake *wider*. All it ever needed was to get swung out
  of Saturn and start falling inward. Gravity does the commuting. You just have
  to catch a falling moon, which is exactly as easy as it sounds."
- **Shepherd fleet:** "Riding shotgun: a shepherd fleet of captured asteroids,
  flying formation as gravity tractors — tugging the moon with nothing but their
  own weight — plus DART-style impactors for trim. It was a heist crew. Every
  heist needs a crew. This one just happened to be made of rocks."

### Playful footnotes (the archivist as fussy straight man — tappable in-UI)
- [1] The committee would like it noted that "yeeting" is now an official
  orbital-mechanics term. The vote was 7–2. The dissenters have been assigned to
  debris-tracking duty.
- [2] "Expected settling" appears 214 times in the official record. The ice
  shell was never interviewed.
- [3] Ten trillion is the conservative estimate. The optimistic estimate is
  worse.
- [4] ARGUS declined to comment, citing "ongoing operations." ARGUS is a
  computer. It does not get to do this. And yet.
- [5] Captain Ren's statement lasted four minutes and contained zero verbs in
  the past tense. Linguists are still arguing about what this means.
- [6] Qiao's 72 hours remain classified. The archivist has filed eleven
  requests. The archivist has received eleven identical photographs of a filing
  cabinet.
- [7] The Roche limit is named for Édouard Roche, who calculated exactly how
  close you can get before everything falls apart, and who presumably never
  imagined his limit would be tested by grand theft moon.
- [8] As of this recording, Mimas has not fragmented. The committee wishes to
  stress the word "recording."

### Tone pitfalls to avoid
- Don't make light of real tragedies; the goof lives in the situation, never in
  anyone's pain. Don't punch at real nations' people — mock bureaucracies and
  euphemisms, never ordinary people or national stereotypes.
- Don't let the goof eat the science: every number is load-bearing; if you
  invent one, footnote that you invented it.
- Ration the bits: "yeet" once per concept; the Death Star comparison twice max
  — then it becomes furniture (furniture is allowed; stop pointing at it).
- Don't rush the heist. Linger on the logistics. Competence is funny — watching
  extremely competent people do an extremely absurd thing is the whole show.

## 8. PRESENTATION / UI NOTES (for the web builder)

**Overall art direction:** "A government archive leaked onto a true-crime fan
wiki at 2am." Deep space-black background with faint starfield grain; documents
as warm off-white paper cards floating in the dark; redaction bars, rubber
stamps, tape strips, yellow "ARCHIVIST'S NOTE" sticky tabs. Accent palette:
**signal orange** (warnings / the Long Torch), **classified teal** (the
Archivist's voice), **alarm red** (contradictions / the Herschel Slip).
Typography: heavy condensed shouty all-caps for documentary title cards;
typewriter mono for transcripts, redaction labels, data readouts; clean readable
body copy so footnotes stay legible. CRT scanlines + vignette ONLY on "recovered
footage" frames — save the grime so it lands.

**Temporal layer chrome (persistent top ribbon + scene badge; never render a
scene without its layer badge; never mix one layer's chrome into another):**
- EVENT: orange-amber ribbon; film-frame chrome with sprocket-hole strip;
  badge e.g. `RECOVERED FOOTAGE — T-DAY −214`.
- RECORD: teal ribbon; clean museum-kiosk panel; folder tabs ("FILE 03 — TOW
  PHASE"); badge `ARCHIVIST'S RECORD — DECLASSIFIED 2049`.
- AFTERMATH: cold blue-violet ribbon; dashed "projection" borders, HUD corner
  brackets, subtle scan sweep; badge `AFTERMATH PROJECTION — +30 YEARS`.
- HEARING: parchment ribbon (or full paper-card inversion); stenographer line
  numbers, `Q.`/`A.` typography, "sworn" stamp; badge `HEARING TRANSCRIPT — DAY 4`.
- Layer transitions: 400ms "rewind/static" wipe — RGB-split glitch, timestamp
  counter spins to the new date, ribbon slides in. Past-ward jumps get a VCR
  rewind treatment (visual only, no autoplay audio).

**POV switching — "channel surfing a declassified broadcast":** persistent POV
tuner bar with three tactile TV-preset buttons — CH-1 CAPT. REN ("THE OFFICIAL
STORY," gold), CH-2 QIAO ("THE FLIGHT RECORD," blue), CH-3 SERA VOSS ("THE
TESTIMONY," red). Tapping tunes with a static burst; scenes marked multi-POV
replay the event from the new channel; unavailable channels show "NO SIGNAL." A
SYNC indicator lights when all three POVs of an event have been viewed.
**TRUST readout — "EXHIBIT T: CREDIBILITY INDEX":** polygraph-style side panel
(desktop) / collapsible drawer (mobile) with three animated bars (never exact
math — vibes, not scores). Hidden until Scene 10. **Contradiction caught:**
three beats — (1) contested line RGB-split glitches, red stamp slams
`CONTRADICTION LOGGED`; (2) both versions pin side-by-side in a "VS" split card
with the delta highlighted (e.g. *burn duration: 9 min* vs *burn duration: 11
min*); (3) the contradiction flies into the **Contradiction Ledger** drawer
(count badge `DISCREPANCIES: n/8`), the liar's trust bar drains, secret-ending
progress ticks up. The ledger is always reviewable — it's the key to THE THIRD
TRUTH.

6. Tone guardrails: goofy-doc, meme-aware, unscary. Thriller bones + real

   body horror. Footnotes carry the jokes; main narrative stays propulsive.
7. Performance: one hero image + SVG/CSS effects; modest page weight for
